Looking Around: All Buildings Are Interesting

“All buildings are interesting. There is not a single building that isn’t interesting in some way.”

In “Looking Around: “All Buildings Are Interesting” Kate Wagner talks about how often we overlook ordinary buildings even though they can tell us a lot about the world around us. She uses the term “interesting” because she says that it doesn’t mean good or bad. Instead every building has something about it that can be looked at, questioned or understood. I questioned if the word “interesting” is the right term to use because it is very subjective. Only because one person finds something interesting doesn’t mean that another person finds it interesting as well, they may find it boring or meaningful. Overall I do agree with her bigger idea but I think a better way to describe it would be with more neutral terms like “Worth noticing” or “significant”. Something does not have to be interesting to everyone to have a meaning or be significant.  For me her idea of paying attention to the ordinary can be applied to more than just buildings. We can use it in different parts of life by paying attention to things we normally overlook for example food, topography, cars, the list is endless. These everyday things can also tell us something about people, culture and the world around us. While I do not completely agree that every building is “interesting”, I do agree that there is value in noticing and looking more closely at things we usually overlook.
